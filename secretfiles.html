第一章Chapter 1最后的一课The Last Lesson

我叫图玛纳寇，是拉西提（Rawhiti）共和国国家研究院的甲级研究员，同时也是中央大学的导师。下面我要讲述的这个故事对于你们来说可能有些遥不可及，甚至听起来就像天方夜谭，但是对于我们拉西提人，甚至哈乌阿鲁(Hauauru)人来说都是无比真实而又沉重的存在。My name is Tumanako. I am a Class-A researcher at the National Research Institute of the Rawhiti Republic, and a lecturer at Central University. The story I am about to tell may sound remote to you — like something out of the <em>Arabian Nights</em>. But for us, the people of Rawhiti, and even for the people of Hauauru, it is real beyond question, and heavy beyond bearing.

故事要从共和国28年11月24日说起。The story begins on the 24th of November, in the twenty-eighth year of the Republic.

我永远都忘不了那个冰冷的早晨，具有讽刺意义的是，那时我正在给孩子们讲物种起源和进化论。这些孩子是共和国恢复高等教育制度后的第一批大学生，他们的眼中充满了对知识、对建设祖国美好明天的渴望。I will never forget that cold morning. The irony is not lost on me: I was standing before my students, lecturing on the origin of species and the theory of evolution. They were the first class of university students enrolled since the Republic restored higher education, and their eyes were bright with hunger — hunger for knowledge, hunger to build a better tomorrow for the motherland.

就在这时，一个教工叩门告诉我阿空迦（Akonga）先生找我，让我务必尽快赶回研究院。这个消息让我欣喜若狂。Then came a knock at the door. A staff member told me that Mr. Akonga wished to see me, and that I should return to the Institute with all haste. The news thrilled me.

阿空迦先生是我的导师，早年在哈乌阿鲁联邦留过学，主修生命工程，现在是研究院的一把手。他是一个不太爱交流的人，因此莫非有极其重大的科学发现，他绝不会主动找我们。Mr. Akonga was my mentor. He had studied abroad in his youth in the Hauauru Federation, specializing in life engineering, and now headed the Institute. He was not a sociable man; nothing short of a momentous scientific discovery would ever move him to seek us out.

那一堂课我是在浮想联翩中度过的，当时的我完全没有想到，这将会是我在中央大学讲台上讲的最后一课。I drifted through the rest of that lecture in a daze of anticipation. It never crossed my mind that it would be the last lecture I would ever give from the podium of Central University.

回到研究院，阿空迦先生已经离开了，留下了满满一实验室的研究成果。研究院的同志告诉我，先生嘱咐我和另外十二名甲级研究员把研究成果打包销毁后再去找他。When I returned to the Institute, Mr. Akonga had already left, leaving behind a laboratory full of research findings. My colleagues told me that he had instructed me and twelve other Class-A researchers to pack up and destroy the findings before going to see him.

先生让我们将研究成果销毁，却没有禁止我们阅读研究报告，甚至我觉得他是特意要让我们这些甲级研究员知道这次实验的结果的。He ordered the findings destroyed — yet he did not forbid us from reading the reports. In fact, I felt he <em>wanted</em> us, his Class-A researchers, to know the outcome of the experiment.

于是，在一整天的伏案研读后，我的世界轰然崩塌。And so, after a full day bent over those pages, my world came crashing down.

第二章Chapter 2树林里的先生The Teacher in the Grove

我是第一个找到阿空迦先生的人，他将自己灌醉在瓦那迦（Whananga）广场后的小树林里，如同一滩烂泥一样望着瓦那迦塑像恸哭。I was the first to find Mr. Akonga. He had drunk himself senseless in the grove behind Whananga Square, slumped like a puddle of mud, wailing before the statue of Whananga.

瓦那迦——进化论的提出者，是生命科学的鼻祖，是共和国的骄傲，他的塑像早在共和国建立之前就矗立在中央大学的广场上，即便是文化运动最厉害的那几年都没有动摇过。Whananga — the father of the theory of evolution, the founding ancestor of life science, the pride of the Republic. His statue had stood in the square of Central University since long before the Republic was founded; not even the fiercest years of the Cultural Movement had shaken it.

“先生！”我心疼地喊道，阿空迦先生一直以来都很注重自己的形象，此刻的他却和过去判若两人，或者说过去的那个他已经死了。‘Sir!’ I cried, my heart aching. Mr. Akonga had always been meticulous about his appearance, but the man before me was a stranger to the man he had been — or rather, the man he had been was dead.

是的，先生已经死了，在我眼前的只是一具被灵魂遗弃的躯壳。他舞动着手臂，阻止了我的搀扶，哭得更加惊天动地了，眼泪被风吹到了我的脸上，像浓酸一样腐蚀着我故作坚强的灵魂。Yes — he was dead. What stood before me was only a husk abandoned by its soul. He waved his arms, refusing my help, and wept all the harder; the wind carried his tears onto my face, where they ate like strong acid into my pretence of fortitude.

“先生！和我回去吧！”我再一次上前拉住他的手，“拉西提的科学家只能流血不能流泪！难道你忘了瓦那迦先生的教诲了吗！”‘Sir! Come back with me!’ I seized his hand once more. ‘A Rawhiti scientist may bleed, but never weep! Have you forgotten the teachings of Whananga?!’

听到这一席话，阿空迦突然就止住了哭泣，茫然地看着我，确切地说是看着我身后的伟人塑像。接着突然放声大笑起来：“他算个什么东西！哈哈哈哈哈，瓦那迦，他就是一个失败者，一个自以为是的大傻瓜！！！哈哈哈哈！！！！”At these words Akonga abruptly stopped crying and stared blankly at me — or rather, past me, at the great man’s statue behind me. Then he burst into wild laughter. ‘What is he, this man?! Ha ha ha ha! Whananga — he was a failure! A self-satisfied great fool!!! Ha ha ha ha!!!!’

瓦那迦是所有学者的精神偶像，如果不是因为我知道了事情的原委，我一定会怒而力争，但是此刻，我跪在阿空迦的面前，扭头看着瓦那迦的塑像，一时之间已经不知道该如何说话了。Whananga was the spiritual idol of every scholar. Had I not known the truth of what had happened, I would have argued in fury. But in that moment, kneeling before Akonga, turning to look at the statue of Whananga, I found I no longer knew what to say.

阿空迦的笑声如同刀子一般扎进了我的听觉神经，并将其连同我的一切思考能力彻底粉碎了。“先生……”我开始忘记了寻找他的真正目的，悲恸如湍流一般席卷而来。Akonga’s laughter stabbed like a knife into my auditory nerves and shattered them along with every capacity I had for thought. ‘Sir…’ I was beginning to forget the true purpose of my search for him, as grief swept over me like a torrent.

阿空迦的眼神涣散了，但这并不妨碍他对我的关爱，他一直以来都是一个慈爱的导师。 “来！”他又一次挣脱了我的手，紧接着将一瓶烈酒递到了我的手里，“为自由干杯！”His eyes had lost their focus, but that did nothing to diminish his affection for me; he had always been a loving mentor. ‘Come!’ He shook off my hand once more and pressed a bottle of strong liquor into it. ‘A toast — to freedom!’

“为自由干杯！”我颤抖地将酒灌体内，感觉一股火焰在我的心中燃烧。‘To freedom!’ I poured the liquor trembling into my body, and felt a fire kindle in my heart.

第三章Chapter 3赤炎神坛The Crimson Altar

共和国29年1月1日，赤炎神坛。The 1st of January, in the twenty-ninth year of the Republic. The Crimson Altar.

圣歌在空中流淌，平静却在心中扎根。A hymn flowed through the air, and peace took root in my heart.

“你是否相信伊沪·玛西亚（Ihu Mesia）是神？”‘Do you believe that Ihu Mesia is God?’

“我相信。”‘I believe.’

阿空迦先生在一袭白袍前虔心而跪。这个一个多月来，他仿佛一下子老了，但他略显苍老的脸庞反而更显智慧。先生是一个坚强的人，在寻找自我的道路上他并没有任何的迷茫，还出色地给我们完成了关于选择的最后一课。Mr. Akonga knelt in devotion before a white-robed figure. In the month and more since I had found him, he seemed to have aged all at once — yet his faintly aged face only looked the wiser for it. He was a strong man; on the road to finding himself he had never wavered, and he had delivered to us, splendidly, his final lesson on the subject of choice.

“你是否相信伊沪·玛西亚为你而死在圣柱上？”‘Do you believe that Ihu Mesia died for you upon the sacred pillar?’

“我相信。”‘I believe.’

在先生的脸上，有两道深深的泪痕，这是真相在他脸上留下的残忍印记，我相信他几乎流尽了所有的眼泪。选择接受固然是痛苦的，但是在我们宣誓成为一位科学工作者的时候，就已经把真理放在了求索的至高点，先生的选择并没有违背我们的誓言。On his face were two deep tracks of tears — the cruel marks the truth had left upon him. I believed he had nearly wept himself dry. To accept is painful, yes; but when we swore our oath as men of science, we had already placed truth at the summit of all our seeking. His choice betrayed nothing of that oath.

“你是否愿意接受伊沪·玛西亚作为你个人的救主？”‘Do you accept Ihu Mesia as your personal saviour?’

“我愿意。”‘I do.’

当说完这句话后，先生抬起了头，他的眼神无比的坚定，再也没有什么能比放下更让人释然。关于那次实验的结果，我们作过了不少的研讨，最终我们十四个人在圣灵的名义下发了誓，决定将这一切永久的埋葬。现在的先生已经浴火重生，准备迎接光明的未来。When he had spoken these words, he raised his head, his gaze utterly resolute; nothing brings such release as letting go. We had deliberated long over the outcome of that experiment, and in the end the fourteen of us swore in the name of the Holy Spirit to bury it all forever. He was reborn through fire now, ready to meet a luminous future.

白袍也望着先生，放下圣典，将手轻轻按在先生的眉头：“我奉圣父圣子圣灵的名为你施洗。”The white-robed figure looked down at him, set aside the holy book, and laid a hand gently upon his brow. ‘I baptize you in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.’

两天后，我和另外几个甲级研究员同样接受了神的洗礼，其中也包括了凯图绔（Kaituku）——这个注定要被整个文明诅咒的人。Two days later, I and several other Class-A researchers received the baptism of God as well — among them Kaituku, the man destined to be cursed by all civilization.

就在这一天，我同时失去了自己曾热爱的两份工作，共和国神圣的教育殿堂里，容不得一个整天将神挂在嘴上的人，再也没有人会在路上喊我同志，我的妻女也在不理解中离开了我。On that same day I lost both the jobs I had loved. In the sacred halls of the Republic’s education, there was no room for a man with God forever on his lips; no one would ever again hail me as ‘comrade’ on the street; and my wife and daughter, unable to understand, left me.

但是这并不重要，因为我已经重生了，自由了。But none of that mattered, for I was reborn. I was free.

也就是在这一天，1月3日， 哈乌阿鲁这头蛰伏许久的猛兽终于朝年轻的共和国露出了它“民主”的爪牙。And on that very day — the 3rd of January — Hauauru, the beast long crouched in wait, finally bared its ‘democratic’ fangs at the young Republic.

战争打响了！War had begun!

第四章Chapter 4两个世界Two Worlds

在这里，我有必要简单介绍一下我们的世界。Here, I should briefly introduce our world.

我们的文明一度极其的分裂，但是就在这一百年里，世界的格局发生了翻天覆地的变化。Our civilization was once deeply fractured, but in the last hundred years the shape of the world has changed beyond recognition.

首先是位于神山以西的哈乌阿鲁的成立，这个奉行民主自由的联邦是由近百个小国组成，由五大政党交替执政。由于技术大爆炸首先在神山以西兴起，哈乌阿鲁在科技方面一度领先，国力也极其强盛。他们不断地对外发动战争来扩大领土，拉西提诸部一度沦为殖民地。First came the founding of Hauauru, west of the Sacred Mountain — a federation of nearly a hundred small states, professing democracy and liberty, governed by five parties in rotation. Because the great technological explosion began west of the mountain, Hauauru long led the world in science and technology, and its national power was immense. It waged war after war to expand its territory, and the tribes of Rawhiti were reduced, for a time, to colonies.

我们拉西提诸部的人民饱受被奴役的痛苦，直到有一天以瓦那迦先生为代表一批学者的横空出世，给拉西提人民带来了曙光。神山以东出现了以解放人民为旗帜的生命党，并以燎原之势在拉西提诸部展开了轰轰烈烈革命运动。终于，在哈乌阿鲁联邦成立52年后，神山以东几乎所有的部族都被解放统一，我的祖国，由生命党一党专政的拉西提共和国成立了。The peoples of the Rawhiti tribes suffered long under the yoke of enslavement — until scholars led by Mr. Whananga appeared as if from nowhere and brought our people the dawn. East of the Sacred Mountain arose the Life Party, flying the banner of liberation, and like wildfire it spread a vigorous revolutionary movement through the Rawhiti tribes. At last, fifty-two years after the founding of the Hauauru Federation, nearly every tribe east of the mountain had been liberated and united, and my motherland was born: the Rawhiti Republic, ruled by the single party of Life.

生命党自成立起就遭到了神山以西的政治家的恶评，这些满怀恶意的言论并没有阻挡住共和国前进的脚步，反而更加激励起人民报效祖国的决心。每个人都将自己视作社会大机器上的一个零件，不知疲倦地劳作着，社会发展的速度居然逐渐赶上了哈乌阿鲁联邦。From its founding, the Life Party was vilified by the politicians west of the mountain. But their venomous words did nothing to halt the Republic’s advance; instead they only steeled the people’s resolve to serve the motherland. Every person saw himself as a cog in the great social machine, labouring tirelessly, and the pace of our development gradually caught up with the Hauauru Federation.

共和国的崛起引起了哈乌阿鲁人的恐慌，于是在五大政党一致投票通过的情况下，这个贪婪的国家决定发起战争，趁着他们不论经济、军事还是科技尚且领先的情况下，一举拿下年轻的共和国。The rise of the Republic spread panic among the Hauauru. And so, with all five parties voting as one, that greedy nation resolved upon war — to crush the young Republic in a single stroke while it still held the advantage in economy, arms, and technology alike.

战争在共和国毫无准备的情况下打响了，百万大军兵分两路从神山的南北两侧攻来，仅用了不到两个月的时间便攻破了边境关卡。接着，联盟的军队风卷残云般席卷了东方七部族，共和军连连败退，在一年的时间里失去了将近四分之一的国土。War erupted while the Republic was utterly unprepared. A million-strong army split in two and struck from the north and south of the Sacred Mountain, breaching the border passes in under two months. Then the alliance’s forces swept like a whirlwind through the Seven Tribes of the East; the Republican army fell back again and again, losing nearly a quarter of the nation’s territory within a year.

在这国家最危难的时刻，几乎每一个共和国的子民都拿起了武器，被征召进了军队。阿空迦先生以及包括我在内的十三位被撤职的甲级研究员也在名列其中。因为信仰了“虚无缥缈”的神，我们的政治地位从顶峰直落谷底，当穿上战袍拿起武器的那一刻，好几个研究员都冤屈地流下了眼泪。In the nation’s hour of greatest peril, nearly every citizen of the Republic took up arms and was conscripted. Mr. Akonga and thirteen dismissed Class-A researchers, myself among them, were no exception. For believing in an ‘insubstantial’ God, our political standing had plunged from the summit to the abyss; and when we donned our uniforms and took up our weapons, several of the researchers wept with the injustice of it.

只有阿空迦先生一人是微笑的，他告诉我们，共和国不健全的制度造就了如今的悲剧，如果生命党继续大材小用、刚愎自用，那即便我们真的沦为亡国奴，那也是神的安排。Only Mr. Akonga smiled. He told us that the Republic’s broken institutions had authored this tragedy — and that if the Life Party persisted in wasting its talent and clinging to its obstinacy, then even should we truly become a conquered people, it would be God’s arrangement.

是的，是神的安排。Yes. God’s arrangement.

第五章Chapter 5塔胡里大捷The Triumph at Tahuri

共和国30年11月03日，这是应该被两个国家共同铭记的日期。The 3rd of November, in the thirtieth year of the Republic — a date both nations should remember.

这一天的傍晚，我们在营帐里一起吃着晚餐。军中的伙食很差，身边的同志们都饿得皮包骨头了。我们这些研究员与阿空迦先生共坐在一张长桌前，因为我们学者身体素质本来就差，很少被派往前线，十三人中只有一人在战火中不幸罹难。That evening we ate dinner together in the mess tent. The army’s rations were poor, and the comrades around me were skin and bones with hunger. We researchers sat with Mr. Akonga at a long table; scholars being of frail constitution, we were seldom sent to the front, and of our thirteen only one had fallen in the flames of war.

大家默默地吃着手中的罐头，突然阿空迦先生放下了餐具。他的眼睛扫过我们每一个人之后，淡淡地说道：“我实在告诉你们，你们中间有一个出卖了我们。”We ate our tinned rations in silence. Suddenly Mr. Akonga set down his utensils. His eyes travelled slowly over each of us, and then he said, quietly, ‘I tell you truly: one among you has betrayed us.’

此话一出，所有人都惊异地说不出话来，因为我们都知道这意味着什么。果然，没多久，前线塔胡里（Tahuri）传来了捷报，共和军大胜联邦军。这是共和军第一次真正意义上的大胜，不但将战线向西整整拉了三百里，更是几乎全歼了联邦军第六装甲师。At these words we all stared, struck speechless, for we knew what they meant. And sure enough, it was not long before triumphant news arrived from Tahuri on the front: the Republican army had routed the Federal forces. It was the first true victory the Republican army had ever won — the front pushed a full three hundred li to the west, and the Federation’s Sixth Armoured Division was very nearly annihilated.

联邦军的第六装甲师在共和国成立之前便已闻名远扬，因为从未有过败绩而被称为“必胜之师”，而此刻联邦军失去的可并不是一个简简单单的头衔，而是整整一个师的兵力！The Sixth Armoured Division had been renowned since before the Republic’s founding; never having known defeat, it was called the ‘Invincible Division.’ And what the Federation had lost was no mere title — it was an entire division of troops!

而联邦军方面呢？居然只动用了一个团的兵力！在这样悬殊的兵力差距下全歼敌方的雄师简直就是白日做梦，我们都知道，有且只有被我们掩埋的那个东西才能造就这样的成功。And on the Federation’s side? They had committed only a single regiment! To annihilate a mighty enemy division against such odds was the stuff of daydreams. We all knew that only one thing — the thing we had buried — could have wrought such a triumph.

众人的目光聚集到了研究员凯图绔的身上，因为在座的人中只有他一个人脸上挂着笑容，露骨得令人发指。All eyes turned to the researcher Kaituku — for of everyone present, he alone wore a smile, so blatant it was revolting.

“为什么！”一个脾气较大的研究员直接便一拳头招呼了上去。‘Why?!’ A hot-tempered researcher swung a fist straight at him.

凯图绔轻而易举地躲开了攻击，眼神中竟然流露出了些许怜悯，他哈哈大笑道：“为什么？！有这么强大的力量放在那儿为什么不用？难道要等联邦的人把我们首都都占领了才后悔么！祖国是怎么教导我们的？！你们就打算眼睁睁看着拉西提人民沦为亡国奴么！”他说得振振有词，大家一时间都语塞了。Kaituku dodged the blow with ease, and there was even a trace of pity in his eyes as he laughed aloud. ‘Why?! With such power lying there, why not use it? Shall we wait until the Federation has taken our capital before we repent? What did the motherland teach us?! Would you stand by and watch the people of Rawhiti enslaved?!’ He spoke with ringing conviction, and for a moment no one could answer.

第十一章Chapter 11凯诺Kino

然而，先生的遗愿恐怕无法实现了，我已经注定成为了我们文明最后一个幸存者，而我并没有自我复制繁衍后代的能力。But his last wish, I feared, could never be fulfilled. I was destined to be our civilization’s last survivor — and I had no power to clone myself and breed descendants.

在布鲁普实验室的门口，心灰意冷的我停下了脚步。At the Bloop Laboratory’s door, my heart turned to ashes, and I stopped.

在布鲁普实验室的门口，凯诺手中的利刃穿过了我的身体。At the Bloop Laboratory’s door, Kino’s blade pierced my body.

我扭过头，看到了他兴奋而扭曲的笑容，文明之花在这绽放的笑容中逐渐凋零。我感到了自己的生命随着流出的鲜血和被注入的毒素一点一滴地夺走，感到了一切对与错正在渐渐融合消逝，感到了喜悦和悲伤反复地上演，感到了世间终极的终极……I turned my head and saw his excited, twisted smile; the flower of civilization withered slowly in that blooming smile. I felt my life draining away drop by drop with the flowing blood and the injected poison; felt all right and wrong slowly merging and dissolving; felt joy and sorrow playing over and over; felt the ultimate of ultimates in this world…

丧尸一直都在人的心里，只要还有希望和梦想，人永远无法成为丧尸。The zombie has always lived in the human heart; so long as hope and dreams remain, a man can never become a zombie.

但是……But…

眼睛再次睁开，我面无表情地将手中的利刃送进了凯诺的身体。他的笑容被无限放大的恐惧所替代。是的，他或者“它”，应该感到恐惧，因为他们无法想象，文明是有多么的坚韧！When my eyes opened again, I drove the blade in my hand into Kino’s body with an expressionless face. His smile was replaced by infinitely magnified terror. Yes — he, or ‘it’, should feel fear, for they could never imagine how tenacious civilization is!

活着的人无法变成丧尸，但是死去的人却可以！我知道凭着我的肉体，早晚会败在凯诺的手里，我只有趁他不注意，才有可能击杀他。而唯一可以让他放松警惕的办法就是死去！其实早在共和国31年5月10日的断头台上，我便早已失去了活下去的动力。The living cannot become zombies — but the dead can! I knew that with this flesh I would sooner or later fall to Kino; only by catching him off guard could I hope to kill him. And the only way to make him drop his guard was to die! In truth, on the guillotine platform on the 10th of May, in the thirty-first year of the Republic, I had already lost all will to live.

这具肉体已经没救了，还不如变成丧尸罢！This flesh was beyond saving — better to become a zombie!

我用神之力在我的大脑里划分了一个全新的区域，在其中存放了我死后以丧尸的形态需要做的事情，接着我便安然接受了自己的死亡，或者是等待自己的新生。而我以丧尸的身份再次睁开眼的时候，做的第一件事，便是将得意忘形的凯诺送下地狱！With the power of God I partitioned a wholly new region in my brain, and in it stored all that I must do after death in zombie form; then I peacefully accepted my death — or awaited my rebirth. And when I opened my eyes again as a zombie, the first thing I did was send the exultant Kino down to hell!

第二十一章Chapter 21基因里的文明Civilization in the Genes

我叫图玛纳寇（Tumanako），曾是拉西提共和国国家研究院的甲级研究员，同时也是中央大学的导师。上面我讲述的那个故事来自于生前的我留下的记忆，这个故事对于作为丧尸的我来说可能毫无意义，但是对于你们，甚至是你们的子嗣都是无比珍贵的警告与参考。My name is Tumanako. I was once a Class-A researcher at the National Research Institute of the Rawhiti Republic, and a lecturer at Central University. The story I have told above comes from memories left by the living me; to me as a zombie the story may be meaningless, but to you — and to your descendants — it is a warning and a reference beyond all price.

这个故事还有两处小小漏洞或不完美，但因为我的记忆片段缺失而无法得到准确的答案，因此我在叙述的过程中一带而过了。但是在这里，我想简单地提一下。There remain two small holes, two imperfections, in this story; missing memory fragments keep me from any certain answer, so I glossed over them in the telling. But here I would like to mention them briefly.

第一，新布鲁普实验室的位置明明是在共和国国家研究院里，但是为什么后来凯诺和救世军是在中央大学找到的？First: the new Bloop Laboratory was plainly housed in the Republic’s National Research Institute — so why did Kino and the Saviours later find it at Central University?

第二，当我和凯诺介绍拉帕玛乌的时候，曾经欲言又止了，生前的我到底影藏了什么机密，作为丧尸的我真的无法臆测。Second: when I was describing Rapa Mau to Kino, I once hesitated on the brink of speech. What secret the living me was hiding, I as a zombie truly cannot conjecture.

这是我在神山之巅的第七天，七天来我的所见所闻远超过去的几十年之和。这原本只在小说中才会出现的山顶景色可以让一切自以为想象力丰富的作家都自惭形秽，因为这里的一切都是我们的世界所没有的，这是一个全新全异的世界！This is my seventh day on the Summit of the Sacred Mountain; what I have seen and heard in these seven days far exceeds the sum of decades past. This summit scenery, fit only for novels, would shame every writer who fancied his imagination rich — for nothing here exists in our world. It is a world wholly, utterly new!

如果阿空迦先生还在世的话，真希望他能借着我的眼睛看到这个世界啊！If Mr. Akonga were still alive — how I wish he could see this world through my eyes!

我驾驶着布鲁普实验室在山顶漫步，利用机械臂捕捉这里的各种生物进行研究，这是生前的我给我留下的任务。这是一个不可能完成的任务，因为这里生物圈的复杂程度远超我的想象，我不知道能否在这具肉体彻底毁坏前完成这个任务。I drive the Bloop Laboratory strolling across the summit, snaring the local creatures with mechanical arms for study — the task the living me left for me. It is an impossible task, for the complexity of the biosphere here far exceeds my imagining; I do not know whether I can complete it before this flesh is utterly ruined.

令我感到惊讶的是，在山顶生物的基因库中，我隐隐约约看到了神之力的影子。或许我并不应该感到惊讶，因为种种迹象表面，山上的生物是由山下的物种演变而来，携带了部分与我们相似的基因片段情有可原。What astonishes me is that in the gene banks of the summit creatures I faintly discern the shadow of the power of God. Perhaps I should not be astonished — for every sign indicates that the mountain’s creatures evolved from the species below, and it is only natural they carry gene fragments resembling ours.

我随机抽取了一些生物，将我们文明的故事用编码的形式记载进了它们的基因库，这些冗余的基因片段不会对这些生物本身造成太多的影响。但是未来的某一天，一旦山顶的某个文明拥有解析基因片段的能力，他们一定会看到我的故事，我们文明的故事。I have sampled creatures at random and written the story of our civilization, in encoded form, into their gene banks; these redundant gene fragments will do the creatures themselves little harm. But some day in the future, once a civilization of the summit gains the power to decode gene fragments, they will surely see my story — the story of our civilization.

这也是生前的我留下的任务，作为丧尸的我因为大脑部分被破坏，思考能力极具有限，无法完全理解这一任务的目的。但是我还是可以大胆地进行一些猜想，如果文明之花可以在神山之巅上绽放，那么它早晚有一天会遭遇和我们文明一样的劫难，“它”会带着丧尸大军再次席卷这个世界，到时我留下的这些信息或许可以给新的文明一些提示，让他们对这注定到来的恐惧做好准备。This too was the living me’s bequest. I as a zombie, my brain partly destroyed, have strictly limited powers of thought and cannot fully grasp this task’s purpose. But I may venture a bold conjecture: if the flower of civilization can bloom upon the Summit of the Sacred Mountain, then sooner or later it will meet the same calamity as ours — ‘it’ will return with its zombie armies to sweep this world again, and then the information I leave may give the new civilization some hint, that they may prepare for the fear that is destined to come.

阿空迦先生说过，战争只是加快了审判的到来，并不是文明毁灭的根源。这不禁让我想到了若干年后，如果山顶上的文明处在和平的年代，能否在这末世劫难中幸存？Mr. Akonga once said that war only hastened the coming of judgement; it was not the root of civilization’s ruin. Which makes me wonder: years hence, if the summit’s civilization dwells in an age of peace — could it survive this apocalyptic doom?

多想亲眼看到这一切啊！但是……恐怕没有这个机会了。How I long to see it all with my own eyes! But… I fear I shall never have the chance.

尾声Epilogue圣阿空迦大教堂St. Akonga Cathedral

公元20XX年，南太平洋某个“不存在”的小岛上。In the year 20XX of the Common Era, on a certain ‘nonexistent’ island in the South Pacific.

一辆黑白相间的劳斯莱斯车无声地停在了东海岸的山路旁，从车里走出了一男一女两人。男人长着一张亚欧混血的脸，微卷的棕发下，一双黑眸如同宇宙一般深邃。他穿着笔挺的西服，胸口上一个看起来像方形太极的徽章在南半球的阳光下熠熠生辉。女人带着黑色的蕾丝眼罩，穿着一身宽松的碎花长裙，她的手优雅地放在微微隆起的腹部上，嘴角洋溢着幸福的笑容。A black-and-white Rolls-Royce came to a silent stop beside a mountain road on the east coast, and from it stepped a man and a woman. The man had a Eurasian face; beneath his loosely curling brown hair, a pair of dark eyes ran deep as the cosmos. He wore a sharply tailored suit, and on his chest a badge like a squared taijitu glittered in the southern-hemisphere sun. The woman wore a black lace blindfold and a loose floral gown; her hand rested gracefully upon her gently swelling belly, and a happy smile played at the corners of her mouth.

“亲爱的，我们到了。”男人从后面轻轻搂住了女子，并亲手揭掉了她的眼罩。‘Darling, we’re here.’ The man embraced her softly from behind and with his own hands lifted away her blindfold.

“噢！我的上帝！”女人的眼中闪过一片璀璨，黑色的瞳孔里，一座雄伟的教堂矗立在蓝天碧海之间，几何切割般的造型带着另类的宗教美传递着祥和与美好。‘Oh! My God!’ Brilliance flashed across the woman’s eyes; in her dark pupils a majestic cathedral stood between blue sky and azure sea, its geometrically faceted form conveying, in an unconventional religious beauty, peace and grace.

“这就是我们的教堂！”男人低头咬着女人的耳朵，声音温柔如水。‘This is our cathedral!’ The man bent and nibbled her ear, his voice gentle as water.

“你真是我的英雄！”女子地清唱了几句赞美诗，之后回头拥抱了自己最爱的男人，“亲爱的，我可以给教堂命名吗？”‘You truly are my hero!’ The woman softly sang a few lines of a hymn, then turned and embraced the man she loved best. ‘Darling, may I name the cathedral?’

“对不起，这个岛上的所有建筑你都可以取名，唯独它不行。”男人将食指轻轻抚平了女子嘟起的小嘴，柔声道：“因为它已经有名字了。”‘Forgive me — every building on this island you may name, except this one.’ The man gently smoothed her pouting lips with his forefinger and said softly, ‘For it already has a name.’

“叫什么呀？”女人迫不及待地问道。‘What is it called?’ the woman asked eagerly.

“圣阿空迦大教堂。”男人抬头看着万里晴空，说道。‘St. Akonga Cathedral.’ The man gazed up at the cloudless sky and said.

“阿空迦是谁？圣经里没有这个人呀！”女人不解地看着男人。‘Who is Akonga? There is no such person in the Bible!’ The woman looked at him, puzzled.

“是啊，圣经里没有这个人，他只存在于神的‘机密档案’中”。男人笑了，语气中多了一丝难以察觉的豪情。‘True, there is no such person in the Bible. He exists only in God’s ‘Secret Files.’’ The man smiled, and in his tone there was a trace of heroism, barely perceptible.

东方的海面上，朝阳将海水染成了血红色，成群的海鸟在空中嘤嘤哀啼。故事没有结束，故事才刚刚开始……On the eastern sea, the rising sun dyed the waters blood-red, and flocks of seabirds cried mournfully in the sky. The story is not over. The story has only just begun…
